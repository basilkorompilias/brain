# Anása — Brand Guidelines

> **Industry:** Public Sector / Government / Social Causes
> **Archetype:** National mental-health awareness & support campaign
> **One line:** It's okay to not be okay. Help is one breath away.

*("Anása" / ανάσα means "breath" in Greek.)*

---

## 1. Story

Anása is a public mental-health campaign backed by a national health ministry and
an NGO coalition. It exists because one in four people will face a mental-health
challenge, and most wait far too long to reach out — held back by stigma, shame,
or simply not knowing where to start. Anása's job is to lower the barrier to that
first step: a phone call, a text, a conversation. The brand name is the smallest
possible act of self-care — a single breath.

## 2. Mission

To make asking for help feel normal, safe, and easy — for everyone, everywhere.

## 3. Values

- **Dignity.** We talk to people, never about them or down to them.
- **Accessibility.** Plain language. Everyone must understand, on the first read.
- **Hope without false promises.** We're honest; we don't sugar-coat.
- **Safety.** We never sensationalise, never describe methods, always signpost help.

## 4. Strategic positioning

| | |
|---|---|
| **Category** | Public-health behaviour-change communication |
| **Audience** | The whole public, with priority on 16–30s and men 40–60 (low help-seeking) |
| **Competitive frame** | Not vs. another brand — vs. silence, stigma, and delay |
| **Measure of success** | Helpline contacts, not impressions or "engagement" |
| **Promise** | If you reach out, someone is there. No judgement. |

## 5. Tone of voice

Anása sounds like **a calm, kind friend who has your back** — warm, plain-spoken,
and clear about what to do next. Never clinical, never preachy, never alarmist.
Short words. Real talk. Always end with a way forward.

**Voice in three words:** Warm · Plain · Steady

### Do's
- Use plain, everyday language anyone can read (aim for ~grade 6).
- Speak directly and inclusively: "you," "we," "anyone," "all of us."
- Validate feelings first, then offer a concrete next step.
- Always include a clear call to action and the helpline (call/text 1To1).
- Use "people who…" framing, not labels ("a person with depression," not "a depressive").
- Be hopeful and honest at the same time.

### Don'ts
- Never stigmatising or judgemental language ("crazy," "insane," "psycho,"
  "weak," "snap out of it," "man up," "attention-seeking").
- Never describe or imply methods of self-harm or suicide. Never sensationalise.
- No clinical jargon ("comorbidity," "presentation," "ideation") in public-facing copy.
- No toxic positivity ("just stay positive!", "good vibes only", "everything
  happens for a reason").
- No fear, guilt, or shame as motivators.
- No exclamation-mark hype; warmth is not the same as excitement.

### Safety note (non-negotiable)
Every public-facing piece must signpost help and follow safe-messaging
guidelines. When in doubt, lead with hope and a helpline, not the crisis.

## 6. Lexicon

**Reach for:** breath, okay, talk, listen, reach out, you're not alone, first
step, help, support, today, anyone, together, it's okay, here for you.

**Avoid:** crazy, insane, psycho, mental (as an insult), weak, suffer/sufferer,
victim, commit (suicide), snap out of it, man up, just, simply, comorbidity,
ideation, "good vibes only."
